# Phat Kaphrao with Thai Basil Mince and Crispy Fried Eggs

*Benne Benzing’s version of the Thai street-food dish: browned mince in a glossy, salty-sweet sauce with Thai basil, jasmine rice and crisp-edged fried eggs. Serves 2; about 30 minutes.*

## Ingredients

### Rice and mince

- 150 g jasmine rice
- 400 g mixed ground meat (beef and pork)
- 1 large shallot
- 3 garlic cloves
- 1 red chilli; for a hotter version, use 2–3 bird’s-eye chillies instead
- 1 bunch Thai basil leaves (25–30 g leaves)
- 2 tbsp neutral, high-heat oil, for the mince

### Sauce

- 2 tbsp oyster sauce
- 1 tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tsp dark soy sauce
- 1 tsp brown sugar
- 3 tbsp water

### Fried eggs

- 2 eggs
- Neutral oil, approximately 1 cm deep in a small frying pan

## Method

1. Rinse the jasmine rice until the water runs clear, then cook it according to the packet instructions.
2. Stir together the oyster sauce, fish sauce, both soy sauces, brown sugar and 3 tbsp water until the sugar dissolves. Keep the sauce beside the stove.
3. Finely chop the shallot, garlic and chilli, or pulse them briefly in a food processor. Do not turn them into a paste.
4. Heat a frying pan or wok until very hot. Add the 2 tbsp oil and the mince; press the mince flat. Leave it undisturbed for 2–3 minutes so the underside browns, then turn and fry until crumbly and cooked through.
5. Reduce the heat to medium, add the chopped shallot mixture and cook for 1–2 minutes, stirring, until fragrant.
6. Pour in the sauce and reduce for 1–2 minutes, until it clings to the meat and looks glossy. If it becomes too dry, add 1–2 tbsp more water.
7. Turn off the heat and fold in the Thai basil leaves. Let them wilt in the residual heat rather than frying them further.
8. Meanwhile, heat about 1 cm of neutral oil in a small pan. Fry the eggs one at a time, spooning hot oil over the whites but not the yolks. Remove when the edges are brown and bubbly and the yolks are still runny; do not flip them. Lower each egg carefully into the hot oil, as it can splatter.
9. Divide the rice and mince between two plates or bowls and top each with a fried egg. Serve immediately.

## Notes

- The video demonstrates one portion; the creator’s caption and written recipe specify the quantities above for two portions.
- Traditional phat kaphrao uses holy basil (kaphrao). Benne uses Thai basil (horapha) because holy basil was unavailable; it has a different, more anise-like flavour. Use holy basil if you can find it.
- The creator says the cooked mince keeps covered in the refrigerator for 1–2 days. Reheat it in a pan, add fresh basil after reheating and fry the eggs fresh.
- The creator does not specify a fixed rice-cooking time or oil volume for the eggs beyond the approximate 1 cm depth.
