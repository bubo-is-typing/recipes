# Overnight Slovak Crêpes (Palacinky)

*Thin Slovak crêpes made from a yeasted batter mixed the evening before. The person who supplied the note clarified that the finished dish is palacinky; the note does not specify a yield or cooking time.*

## Ingredients

- 1 egg
- 1–2 tbsp sugar
- A scant ½ tsp salt
- 1 tsp dry yeast
- Less than ½ tsp baking powder (the original describes more than a knife-tip but less than half a small coffee spoon)
- 500 g flour (type not specified)
- Milk, as needed for a smooth, pourable crêpe batter (amount not specified)
- 1½ tbsp oil (type not specified)
- A glass of sparkling water, added at the end (glass size not specified)

## Method

1. Mix the egg, sugar, salt, dry yeast and baking powder.
2. Add the flour, then add milk gradually so the mixture can be stirred. Mix thoroughly until smooth.
3. Mix in the oil and add more milk until the batter has a pourable crêpe-batter consistency. Finally, stir in a glass of sparkling water; the original uses this in addition to, not instead of, the milk.
4. Let the batter rest briefly. For an overnight rest, cover it and refrigerate promptly at 4°C or colder. The original says to leave it in the kitchen briefly, then on the balcony overnight; see the safety note below before following that storage instruction.
5. In the morning, bring the batter into the kitchen before cooking, then cook it as thin crêpes when needed. The original does not specify how long to warm it, the pan temperature, cooking time, size or number of crêpes.

## Notes

- **Dish identified by the person who supplied the note:** The Slovak note calls the consistency *palacinkové cesto*; they clarified that these are thin Slovak crêpes (*palacinky*), not fluffy American-style pancakes. No serving count or exact milk quantity is provided.
- **Overnight food safety:** Because the batter contains raw egg and milk, use a refrigerator at 4°C or colder rather than an unmonitored balcony for its overnight rest. Keep the brief room-temperature rests within a total of 2 hours (1 hour if above 32°C), then cook the batter thoroughly. This is an editorial safety adaptation, not what the original note literally instructs. Do not taste the raw batter.
- The words “a glass” and “a little while” are kept deliberately imprecise; the original does not define these quantities or a proofing time. The flour type and oil type are also unspecified.
